1

🎥 Understand the problem: from raw footage to reel

You record a video raw several minutes long: halting speech, silences, a “let me repeat that,” a glass of water midway through. Today, turning that into a good reel means hours in an editor or paying for an edit — which often turns out weaker than you wanted. Forja Reel handles that part: you provide the raw footage and get a 60–90s reel ready to publish, without opening a video editor.

New here? — “raw footage” and “reel”

Raw footage is the raw recording, uncut, just as it came from the camera/phone. Reel is the short, vertical (9:16) ~15–90s video that runs on Instagram, TikTok, and Shorts. The whole job is to turn the first into the second.

What it is

Turn a long raw video into a short reel, without a manual editor.

Why learn

You deliver professional-level editing in minutes, on your own schedule and without relying on others.

Key concepts

Raw footage, reel, automatic editing, immediacy.

2

🧩 Meta-Skill vs. Skill: It Generates Your Editor

Here’s the twist that confuses a lot of people: Forja Reel doesn’t edit your video. It does one thing beyond that—it interviews you and builds a custom editor for you. This generated editor is what later edits your videos. That’s why it’s a meta-skill: a skill that creates another skill.

New here? — “skill,” “meta-skill,” and “child skill”

A skill is a skill you install in the assistant (Claude) so it can perform a task—here, “edit reel.” A meta-skill doesn’t execute the task: it factory a new skill. The skill that comes out of the box is the child skill — o your editor, with your name and your look.

Standard skill

Does ONE thing the same way for everyone. E.g.: "cut this video." The result is the same for anyone who installs it.

Meta-skill (Forja Reel)

Asks who you are and GENERATES a new skill with your identity. Two people end up with editors different from the same meta-skill.

Why learn: understand that you receive a your own editor — not a generic service — it changes how you think about the result: you can adjust, version, and reuse it.

Key concepts: skill, meta-skill, child skill.

3

⚖️ Engine vs. identity: what’s common and what’s yours

The child skill has two clearly separate halves. One is the engine — the technical part that makes any reel good: clean cuts, pacing, anti-AI review. This half is shared, the same for everyone. The other is the identity — colors, how you appear on screen, pacing, type of B-roll, cold open. This half is only yours and leaves the interview.

🔑 The golden rule

Share the engine, never the style. That's why two videos made with Forja Reel don't look alike: everyone inherits what's good (the engine); each person builds their own identity.

🔧 Engine (Shared)

  • •Automatic clean cut
  • •Measured pacing (never 4s with no motion)
  • •Reviewer that avoids the "AI look"

🎨 (Your) identity

  • •Your brand colors and typography
  • •How you appear (full screen, PiP…)
  • •Preferred pacing, B-roll, and cold open

Key concepts: engine, identity, profile (the summary of your answers that becomes your half).

4

✨ The result it delivers: pro level

The finished reel isn’t just “cut.” It comes with four ingredients that usually cost a lot and take a lot of manual work: motion graphics, Real B-roll, captions e SFX. Understanding what each one does helps you ask for what you want and assess whether it turned out well.

✦

Motion graphics

Text and elements that animate on screen — a word that pops in, a number that counts up. It adds rhythm and highlights what matters.

✦

Real B-roll

Real supporting visuals (an actual capture of the site/repo you mentioned), not generic stock imagery. It adds credibility.

✦

Captions + SFX

Captions at the right height (so they don’t disappear under the app bar) and sound effects (whoosh, boom) that “produce” the cut. This detail separates amateur from pro.

Key concepts: motion graphics, B-roll, captions, SFX. Each gets its own definition in the glossary (topic 7).

5

⏱️ Record in 15, get the result in 15: immediacy has its rewards

Recording in ~15 minutes and having the reel ready in ~15 minutes changes the game: any news story or idea becomes content on the same day, while the topic is still hot. A human editor would have a hard time delivering within that window — and speed is exactly what social networks reward.

💡 Why this matters

The algorithm rewards people who publish at the right time and often. Closing the record→publish loop in half an hour keeps you react to what happens, not just plan weeks in advance.

Key concepts: speed, opportunity (topic window), cost.

6

🤖 Make It Not Look AI-Generated

A strong rule for the engine: the reel must not look like an automatic template. What engages viewers is looking human and intentionally edited. That's why the engine avoids the typical clichés of AI-generated content.

✓ What the engine looks for

  • ✓Cuts with intention, varied pacing
  • ✓Real B-roll specific to the topic
  • ✓Text that supports what you say instead of repeating it

✗ The AI cliché (avoid)

  • ✗"THE PROBLEM / THE SOLUTION" capsules
  • ✗Generic captions on every line
  • ✗Chapter labels and default transitions

New here? — “look-IA” (or “AISLOP”)

It’s that look that immediately gives away that the video was made from an automated template: always the same appearance, the same labels, the same rhythm. Avoiding it is what makes your reel look “edited by a person.”

Key concepts: AI look, cliché, curation.

7

📚 Foundational Glossary

The six terms that recur throughout the course, defined in plain language. No orphaned jargon: from here on, when one appears, you’ll know what it means.

Reel
Short vertical video (9:16), ~15–90s, published on Instagram/TikTok/Shorts.
B-roll
A supporting image or clip that appears over the spoken audio to illustrate what you’re saying.
Cold open
The first 1–3s of impact that “hook” viewers before any introduction.
Motion graphics
Animated text and graphic elements on screen to add rhythm and emphasis.
SFX
Sound effects (whoosh, boom, ching) that accentuate cuts and transitions.
Render
The final process that “flattens” all the layers into a single video file (MP4).
