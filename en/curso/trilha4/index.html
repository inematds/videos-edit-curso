🎯 The 6 Recipes + Cold Open

The menu of treatments the engine applies: how you appear, when B-roll comes in, split screen, punchy text—and the cold open that kicks it all off.

What it is:

The basic recipe: you speaking full-screen, with real B-roll layered in at the right moments. It’s the most versatile treatment and works for almost any reel.

Why learn:

It’s the default you’ll use most; mastering when B-roll comes in keeps you from having a static “talking head.”

Key concepts:

Full screen, B-roll overlay, base recipe.

What it is:

Picture-in-picture: you stay on screen while a smaller window shows the B-roll at the same time. Your face and supporting footage share the same frame.

Why learn:

Keeps you connected with the speaker while illustrating—good when your reaction matters as much as the supporting visuals.

Key concepts:

PiP, floating window, coexistence.

What it is:

You start full screen, and when it’s time to demonstrate, your face becomes a small PiP and the shared screen takes over the frame. Ideal for showing something in practice.

Why learn:

It’s the tutorial/demo formula: viewers see what you do without losing sight of you.

Key concepts:

Screenshare, face→PiP transition, demo.

What it is:

The screen is split: you on one side, the supporting footage on the other (or before×after, option A×B). Both occupy half the frame at the same time.

Why learn:

Comparisons are obvious when they’re side by side; that’s the recipe for contrast.

Key concepts:

Split, comparison, visual contrast.

What it is:

Motion graphics: the keyword hits with impact (scale, weight, SFX) the moment you say it. It’s not a generic caption—it’s intentional emphasis.

Why learn:

Keeps the eye on what matters and delivers the “visual punch” that holds attention at a pace of ≤4s.

Key concepts:

Motion graphics, punch, highlighted word.

What it is:

Instead of a generic image, the recipe inserts an actual screenshot or clip of what you mention—the repository, the site, the product—often inside a browser frame.

Why learn:

The real footage builds credibility and holds attention better than a stock image icon.

Key concepts:

Real capture, browser frame, proof.

What it is:

The required opening: the first 0–3s hook viewers before any introduction — with the actual result, a strong hook, or an interesting fact.

Why learn:

It’s where the reel lives or dies: without a hook in the first 3s, no one watches the rest.

Key concepts:

Cold open, hook, opening hook.

🎬 Real end-to-end workflow + QC

The step-by-step process applied to a real video: beat sheet, real B-roll, generated SFX, reviewer loop, and the final checklist before publishing.

What it is:

A real raw video going through the engine’s 5 phases—cutting, analysis+treatment, motion+B-roll, SFX+render+QC, and review—with a single command.

Why learn:

Seeing the whole workflow brings together everything you studied in the earlier tracks in a single result.

Key concepts:

5 phases, end-to-end workflow, single command.

What it is:

A map of the video segments and the pacing for each one: brisk for hooks and lists, with breathing room for dense concepts. It’s the editing plan before you edit.

Why learn:

Pacing is no longer guesswork: each segment gets the treatment and speed that fit it.

Key concepts:

Beat sheet, pacing by segment, editing plan.

What it is:

When you mention a repository or website, Firecrawl fetches the actual capture and the engine inserts it in a browser frame at the right beat.

Why learn:

It’s the difference between “talking about something” and “showing it”—much more convincing.

Key concepts:

Firecrawl, real capture, insert.

What it is:

Sound effects under the voice: a whoosh sweeps through the transition and lands on a boom at the section cut. No music by default—just occasional SFX.

Why learn:

It’s the inexpensive detail that makes the reel sound “produced” instead of raw.

Key concepts:

SFX, whoosh, boom, section cut.

What it is:

An independent reviewer watches the reel and gives a PASS or FAIL: it looks for repetition, overlap, and sync issues. If it fails, it fixes them and repeats until it passes.

Why learn:

It’s quality assurance that runs on its own—you get a reel that’s already been reviewed.

Key concepts:

Reviewer, PASS/FAIL, quality loop.

What it is:

The checklist every reel needs to pass: pacing gate, nothing >4s without a visual hit, text at chest/mic height, resolution preserved, and nothing that looks AI-generated. Includes a ready-to-paste review prompt.

Why learn:

It’s the final gate: if any item fails, it doesn’t publish—you fix it first.

Key concepts:

Checklist, gate, review prompt.
